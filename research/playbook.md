# AI-Powered SEO Content Production Playbook

## 1. Executive Summary

This playbook presents a practical workflow for using AI to support SEO content production while maintaining search intent, content quality, originality, and human oversight.

The recommendations in this playbook are based on research from 10 SEO and content marketing practitioners.

## 2. Objective

The objective is to create an efficient, repeatable SEO content production process that uses AI for research and production support without sacrificing accuracy, usefulness, or originality.

## 4. Recommended AI-Powered SEO Content Production Workflow

### Step 1: Define the Searcher's Problem

Start by identifying what the audience is actually trying to accomplish with the search query.

Before creating content, determine:

- The primary search intent
- The audience's problem or question
- The expected type of content
- What a useful answer should contain

I would not begin by asking AI to write an article immediately. The content strategy should come before content generation.

### Step 2: Research the Topic and Keywords

Research the target topic, related queries, competing content, and important subtopics.

The goal is not simply to find keywords with high search volume. The research should identify the questions, subtopics, and information users expect to find.

AI can assist with organizing research and identifying potential subtopics, but important facts and search data should be independently verified.

### Step 3: Build a Content Brief

Create a structured brief before generating the draft.

The brief should contain:

- Primary topic
- Primary keyword
- Search intent
- Target audience
- Important subtopics
- Questions to answer
- Suggested content structure
- Internal linking opportunities
- Information that requires expert or primary sources

This creates a clear direction for the AI instead of asking it to generate content from a vague prompt.

### Step 4: Use AI for Drafting and Production Support

Use AI to accelerate repetitive parts of the production process.

AI can assist with:

- Creating an initial outline
- Expanding sections
- Generating alternative explanations
- Summarizing research
- Creating title and heading variations
- Identifying missing questions
- Creating content briefs

AI output should be treated as a draft rather than automatically publishable content.

### Step 5: Add Human Review and Original Value

Review the AI-generated material for:

- Accuracy
- Search intent
- Factual claims
- Repetition
- Generic language
- Unsupported statements
- Originality
- Usefulness to the reader

Add original examples, practical experience, analysis, or expert input wherever possible.

This is an important control point in the workflow because faster content production does not automatically mean better content.

### Step 6: Optimize the Content

After the content is reviewed, optimize it for both users and search engines.

Check:

- Title
- Headings
- Search intent alignment
- Internal links
- Relevant terminology
- Readability
- Meta description where appropriate
- Images and supporting media where useful

Optimization should improve the usefulness and discoverability of the content rather than forcing keywords into the text.

### Step 7: Fact-Check Before Publishing

Before publication, verify important claims against reliable sources.

Pay particular attention to:

- Statistics
- Dates
- Product information
- Technical claims
- Quotes
- Research findings
- Claims about Google or search engine behavior

Any claim that cannot be confidently verified should be removed, qualified, or researched further.

### Step 8: Publish and Measure

After publication, monitor performance rather than assuming the content succeeded.

Track relevant signals such as:

- Organic impressions
- Click-through rate
- Organic traffic
- Rankings for important queries
- Engagement
- Conversions where applicable

Use performance data to identify content that needs improvement.

### Step 9: Update and Improve

SEO content should be treated as an ongoing asset.

When performance or information changes:

- Update outdated information
- Improve weak sections
- Add useful information
- Refresh examples
- Strengthen internal links
- Reassess search intent

The goal is to create a continuous research → production → measurement → improvement cycle.

## 5. Production Checklist

Before publishing any AI-assisted SEO article, I would check:

- [ ] Search intent is clearly defined.
- [ ] The target audience is understood.
- [ ] Keyword and topic research has been completed.
- [ ] A content brief exists.
- [ ] AI was used as a production aid rather than the sole source of expertise.
- [ ] Important factual claims were verified.
- [ ] The content provides original value.
- [ ] The article answers the searcher's main question.
- [ ] Internal linking opportunities were considered.
- [ ] The content was reviewed by a human.
- [ ] The final version is useful and readable.
- [ ] A plan exists to measure performance after publication.
      
## 6. Where Experts Disagree

### Disagreement 1: How aggressively should AI automate SEO content production?

**What Julian Goldie recommends**

Julian Goldie presents a highly automated approach to SEO. His June 2026 YouTube video demonstrates an AI SEO agent connected to Google Search Console that can identify keywords and automatically deploy content. His LinkedIn post also emphasizes Google's AI agents and creating content that can work in AI search.

Source: Julian Goldie, YouTube video, "How I Built an AI SEO Agent to Rank #1 in 10 Minutes (Claude Code)" — https://youtu.be/69PT1AUxlOw?si=KIrXeHEBUpnW9mSz


Source: Julian Goldie, LinkedIn post, "Google changed SEO forever in 25 minutes" — https://www.linkedin.com/posts/juliangoldieseo_google-changed-seo-forever-in-25-minutes-share-7473742827709976576-McNN/


**What Mark Williams-Cook recommends**

Mark Williams-Cook takes a much more cautious view of AI-driven search. In his 2025 discussion, he highlights hallucinations, the risks of AI-generated information, and uncertainty around how AI will affect traditional search. His discussion makes aggressive automation look risky when accuracy and trustworthy information are important.

Source: Mark Williams-Cook, YouTube video, "SEO Mark Williams-Cook on AI vs Google Search....Behind the Scenes" — https://youtu.be/8humkUT8w9k?si=4pKUgNSg2mMi4nCp

**Which side I take and why**

I would take a middle position.

I would use AI aggressively for research, outlining, repetitive production tasks, and workflow automation, but I would not allow an AI agent to publish content without human quality control.

The reason is that automation can increase production speed, but the research also shows that AI systems can produce unreliable information. Therefore, I would automate the workflow but keep human approval as the publishing gate.

### Disagreement 2: Should SEO start with individual keywords or broader topics and intents?

**What Brian Dean's research suggests**

Brian Dean's research emphasizes traditional SEO concepts such as on-page optimization, keyword targeting, and attempting to rank for specific keywords.

Source: Brian Dean, LinkedIn post, "How AI is ACTUALLY changing SEO" — https://www.linkedin.com/posts/brianedean_most-people-are-missing-how-ai-is-actually-activity-7446839485004161024-ot6g


**What Aleyda Solis recommends**

Aleyda Solis places more emphasis on understanding broader search intents and topics. In her LinkedIn research, she discusses Bing's AI visibility reporting, including intent categories and grouping related grounding queries into broader thematic topics.

Her YouTube research also emphasizes optimizing feeds and the data provided to search engines and AI systems.

Source: Aleyda Solis, LinkedIn post, "Bing is releasing New AI Visibility Insights in Bing Webmaster Tools" — https://www.linkedin.com/posts/aleyda_bing-is-releasing-new-ai-visibility-insights-share-7472684889935548417-tSt6

Source: Aleyda Solis, YouTube video, "The top SEO tip for 2026 of +12 SEO specialists" — https://youtu.be/al-FuoXXQCs?si=FoUTUJYj-zNHgIE9


**Which side I take and why**

I would prioritize search intent and topic coverage while still using individual keywords as inputs.

Keywords remain useful for discovering demand, but optimizing only around one exact keyword can miss related questions and the broader problem the searcher is trying to solve.

My recommended workflow is therefore:

Keyword research → intent classification → topic/subtopic mapping → content creation.

### Disagreement 3: Is AI search optimization mainly a new discipline or an evolution of SEO?

**What Tim Soulo's research suggests**

Tim Soulo's research presents AI search as an important development that requires measurement and analysis. His research at Ahrefs analyzed large amounts of data around AI search and examined how AI Overviews change over time.

Source: Tim Soulo, LinkedIn post, "In the last 6 months at Ahrefs, we analyzed over 1 billion data points across 14 studies" — https://www.linkedin.com/posts/timsoulo_in-the-last-6-months-at-ahrefs-we-analyzed-activity-7467561528830078976-rUjs

Source: Tim Soulo, YouTube video, "Top SEO myths debunked with real data" — https://youtu.be/ejylKNKAQ4s?si=_-MxWQuQmDY9MfCg


**What Lily Ray's research suggests**

Lily Ray takes a more skeptical approach to the terminology surrounding AI search. Her MozCon presentation specifically discusses GEO, AEO, and LLMO and separates actionable strategies from industry hype. Her position suggests that many of these new labels should be understood in the context of existing SEO principles rather than automatically treated as completely separate disciplines.

Source: Lily Ray, YouTube video, "GEO, AEO, LLMO: Separating Fact from Fiction & How to Win in AI Search" — (https://youtu.be/2nJkT8zOzcM?si=Y-3C8lsJ_wqcmJIn)


**Which side I take and why**

I would treat AI search optimization as an evolution of SEO rather than a completely separate discipline.

The search environment is changing, so marketers need new measurements and workflows. However, the fundamentals of understanding users, creating useful content, building authority, and making information accessible remain relevant.

Therefore, I would adapt the existing SEO process for AI search rather than create a completely separate process from scratch.
